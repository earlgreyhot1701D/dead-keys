# Dead Keys: Curriculum Research

Researched Nov 7, 2026. Each source checked against the primary page, not a summary. Feeds the lesson ladder, the in-app "Why these lessons" page, and the onboarding copy.

## Rules for the lesson ladder (derived)

1. Accuracy first. A lesson is passed on accuracy, not speed.
2. One finger owns each key. The on-screen keyboard shows which.
3. Eyes on the screen. The on-screen keyboard is the reference, not your hands.
4. Short and daily. A session is a few minutes. The game says so.
5. Expect the dip. Speed may fall before it rises. Milestones celebrate accuracy and consistency too, not only WPM.
6. Structure plus play. Lessons teach, games practice, the heat map decides what is next.

## Claims to avoid

- "Typing games are proven to work." Source 7 says structured lessons beat free games.
- "People type fast on phones." Source 4 says phones average about 38 WPM.
- "Touch typing is required to be fast." Sources 2 and 3 say consistency matters more than ten fingers.
- Any MasterType lesson count until a primary source confirms it.

## Not verified, do not use

- News coverage of "Gen Z can't type" (Baylor Lariat, WION, Yahoo). Anecdotal. The only hard number they carry is the NCES figure, which is cited directly above.
